# Commercial Product Analysis — Key Findings

## Deal-Closer Identified

Feature 2 (WhatsApp supplier collection) is the only feature that drives purchase decisions. The CFO buys revenue protection (H&M Scope 3 requirement), not ESG software. All other features support the sale but don't close it. This reframes the build priority: supplier collection first, framework mapping second.

## Competitive Moat

Two genuine USPs exist: WhatsApp-native supplier collection (no competitor does this) and per-data-point confidence tagging (legally risky but defensible). Framework mapping is table stakes. Real-time monitoring has high demo value but low buying value. Implementation speed is a claim, not a moat.

## Bangladesh Market Reality

The 3-6 week implementation claim doesn't survive contact with on-premise SAP B1 in Dhaka. CSV upload must be the primary data path, not ERP API integration. Smart meters may mean digital displays with SMS, not Modbus-capable devices. The product must work with minimal IT infrastructure.

## Coverage > Response Rate

Scope 3 value depends on which suppliers respond, not how many. 20 Tier 1 suppliers covering 80% of spend at 80% response rate is more valuable than 140 suppliers at 40% response. The product should optimize for coverage-weighted response, not raw count.

## Product-Market Misalignment

The brief's "one KPI, multiple frameworks" story is investor-facing. The CFO only needs GRI format for H&M today. Multi-framework output is Year 2 optionality. The product story must lead with "H&M Scope 3 requirement solved" not "CSRD/ISSB/GRI/TCFD compliance."

## Build Estimate

12 autonomous sessions to full commercial product across 4 phases: Data Foundation (3) → Deal-Closer (4) → Proof (3) → Wow (2). Each phase ships independently — the product is demo-able after Phase B, sellable after Phase C.
